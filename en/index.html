Section 3

Risk Assessment

The Risk Assessment meeting

•What is a Risk Assessment? The planes that did not return
  1. In the Second World War, planes returning from missions are examined.
  2. Many bullet holes are seen on the fuselage and the wings.
  3. The first idea: the places with holes are reinforced so that the planes do not go down.
  4. A scientist (Abraham Wald) points to the planes that did not return.
  5. The planes that did not return were hit elsewhere (engine, cockpit) and went down.
  6. Those are the places that really need reinforcing.

NoteRisk Assessment: before a problem occurs, where the product can fail is identified and action is taken; hidden risks are also considered.

Tap the image to enlarge.

•Risk Assessment meeting: when, where, who attends?
  1. A Risk Assessment meeting is held for every style.
  2. It is held before the second fit sample is sent to the customer.
  3. The meeting is preferably held at the production site.
  4. If it cannot be held at the production site, it is held online.
  5. Meeting attendees:
  6. Spring → QC/Manager, GT/Manager, Merchandiser, FT
  7. Supplier → SCQC, Merchandiser, Production, Quality and Sample Room Managers
  8. SCFT if there is one, otherwise the Fabric Planning Lead

Tap the image to enlarge.

•Risk Assessment meeting: topics covered
  1. Yarn and fabric risks, test results, test plan and test requirements
  2. Trims, labels and other components
  3. Packing, folding, shipping method and carton specifications
  4. Style and construction details; comments on previous samples
  5. Measuring method, measurement deviations and tolerances
  6. Style-specific processes: garment dyeing, embroidery, heavy wash
  7. Product safety requirements for children’s and baby products
  8. Production site

•Risk Assessment meeting: after the meeting
  1. Meeting notes are recorded in the Risk Assessment notes in CRM.
  2. The notes are shared with everyone who attended the meeting.
  3. If needed, the outcomes are shared with the customer and approvals are obtained before production.
  4. The identified risks are addressed one by one at the PPM/Mini Pilot/Pilot.

•Line data is checked!

  2. The daily target is compared with the actual output.
  3. It is checked whether the order will meet the shipment date.
  4. When needed, the Spring team is informed.

•Cutting, printing and embroidery!
  1. Cut, printed, embroidered and embellished panels are compared with the approved artwork.
  2. Are placement, colour and handfeel the same as the reference?
  3. If the process is in-house, it is also reviewed on site.
  4. Defective panels are set aside and recorded.
  5. Checks are done with the approved first piece in hand.

!For all processes such as printing, embroidery and hand work, production does not start until the first piece is approved.

•In-line check on the sewing line
  1. The first 5 garments of each station are checked.
  2. At least 1 round is done per day; if there is time, in-line checks continue.
  3. All quality points are checked in every round.
  4. Example: stitch quality, workmanship, symmetry, measurements, thread tension.
  5. Reports are uploaded to the Spring system and/or the customer systems.

Tap the image to enlarge.

•In-line check: when a defect is found!
  1. The defect is shown to the operator.
  2. Work does not move to the next station until it is done right.
  3. A defect is not only caused by the operator.
  4. It can also come from the machine, the fabric, the wrong needle or the wrong thread.
  5. A different action is taken for each cause; production continues with the defect prevented.
  6. An operator who keeps making defects is retrained or moved to another operation.

!Production does not continue until the defect found is corrected.

Tap the image to enlarge.

•Press-fastened button, rivet and snap setting check!
  1. Setting reports for press-fastened buttons, rivets and snaps are reviewed.
  2. The correct die and the correct pressure setting are used.
  3. A pull test is done at the start of each shift and at every setting change.
  4. The attached trim sits firmly; it does not spin, move or leave a sharp edge.
  5. The fabric is not damaged at the attachment point; reinforcement is used if needed.

!A press-fastened trim that comes off is a critical defect, especially on a children’s garment.

•AQL: Acceptance Quality Limit
  1. AQL is the highest defect rate that can be accepted in a lot.
  2. Not the whole lot, but the sample size read from the table is inspected (example: 1,201–3,200 pcs, Level II: 125 pcs).
  3. Defects are classified as critical, major and minor; each class has its own acceptance number.
  4. If the number of defects found exceeds the acceptance number, the lot is rejected.
  5. The Spring standard is Level II, Major 2.5, Minor 4.0. It can vary by customer; if the customer requires a stricter standard, that standard is used.

14The garments are passed through the metal detector!
  1. First the 9-point test: with a 1.2 mm test card at top, middle, bottom × left, centre, right.

  5. All garments are passed through the metal detector and escorted while being passed.
  6. Calibration, operator certificates and hourly test records are checked.

16Colour, handfeel and fabric weight are checked!
  1. Colour is compared with the approved colour standard under D65 in the light box.
  2. Then metamerism is checked under TL84.
  3. D65 and TL84 are the general rule; primary and secondary light sources vary by customer.
  4. Garments are placed side by side to check for shade differences between sizes and cartons.
  5. Handfeel is compared with the approved bulk hanger.
  6. Fabric weight is calculated as 100 cm² circle × 100 = g/m² and compared with the spec.

!A clear colour difference is not accepted without a colour approval.

•Children’s product safety: why does it matter?
  1. A child cannot recognise danger or protect itself.
  2. A small defect in the product can cause serious injury, even death.
  3. A safety defect on a children’s product is a critical defect; critical defects are not accepted.
  4. A safety defect leads to the product being recalled from stores.
  5. If the defect is not caught at the Final Inspection, it reaches the child.

Tap the image to enlarge.

•Children’s products: safety checks!
  1. Does the neck stretch to the minimum measurement?
  2. Do cord and tie lengths comply with the rule?
  3. Is the snap set into the same number of fabric layers?
  4. Is the interlining under the embroidery clean? Are there long or untrimmed threads?

!A safety defect on a children’s product is a critical defect. A single critical defect fails the order.

  1. As soon as a defect is found, a meto sticker is put right on top of it.
  2. Defective garments are put in a separate place from the garments still to be inspected.
  3. Garments with critical, major and minor defects are kept separately.
  4. Good garments go on the hanger, defective garments go on the defect table.

!If a defective garment without a meto sticker gets mixed with the good ones, the report result can be affected.

31Photos are taken at every stage and added to the report while it is written
  1. Photos are taken at every stage, from the finished goods warehouse to the defect table.
  2. One shot is taken for each defect; a close-up is added if needed.
  3. The defect sticker and the tape measure must be visible.
  4. The photo is named with the step, carton no., size and defect information.
  5. While the report is written, the photo is added next to the related finding.

NoteThe photo is taken neither too close nor too far; the defect and its area are visible together.

•Spring Fail inspection rules
  1. Any critical defect, or major/minor over the acceptance number: Fail.
  2. Fit not matching the Gold Seal: Fail; the GT is informed.
  3. After a Fail, shipment stops; the report is published within the day.
  4. The factory inspects 100%, then the SCQC re-inspects.
  5. Re-inspection fee: min. 75 GBP for the 2nd, 125 GBP for the 3rd and later.
  6. Alternatively, the inspection is organised with an independent company.
  7. No fee is charged for packing Fails.

•Final inspection room: summary (Steps 15–32)!
  1. Gold Seal: Production is compared side by side with the Gold Seal; a fit check is done.
  2. Colour, handfeel, fabric weight: D65 in the light box, then TL84.
  3. Polybag label: Barcode, size, colour, warning; the polybags are removed.
  4. Folding: After removal from the polybag, the fold is checked; each size is photographed.
  5. Separated and hung up: Size by size, all facing the same way.
  6. Swing tags and labels: Swing tag, size and care label are checked while on hangers.
  7. Hanging appearance: A garment that looks different is set aside and measured.
  8. Measurement: 3 per size; if out of tolerance, increased to 5.
  9. Width / length: Garments are laid out on the table size by size; differences are seen.
  10. Clockwise: Same order on every garment: no point is skipped.
  11. Symmetry: Are left and right equal? If in doubt, fold and measure.
  12. Inside check: The garment is turned inside out; seams, overlock and labels are checked.
  13. Trims: Zip, button, snap: opened and closed 3 times on every garment. Test reports are reviewed.
  14. Stretch: Neck and waist are stretched; does the seam crack?
  15. Classification and agreement: Classified, compared with AQL, shown to the factory, agreed.
  16. Defects are set aside: Marked with stickers; critical / major / minor kept separately.
  17. Photos: Taken at every stage; every finding goes into the report with its photo.
  18. Report is published: Published within the day; the customer representative is called.

!Out-of-tolerance counts towards Fail. One critical defect fails the order. Report within the day.
